The Conversation

A short story from the world of Dynamo Bresno · About 6 minutes

← Short Stories

The rain arrived without warning, turning the streets of Bresno into rivers of reflected neon. Viktor Kosa sat alone in the back of a black sedan, watching the city slide past the tinted glass. At thirty-two, the captain of Dynamo Bresno still carried himself like a centre-back who expected trouble around every corner. Broad shoulders. Scar across his jaw. Eyes that never settled. Officially, he was attending a charity dinner. Officially, nobody knew he had crossed the River Valrekia after midnight. Officially, he wasn’t considering leaving Dynamo Bresno at all.

The official story rarely survived long in Valrekia. The driver said nothing. He wore dark gloves despite the summer heat. They crossed into East Bresno, through the industrial district, where steel towers pierced the low clouds and illuminated advertisements flickered like coded messages, and then on, through the habited zones, each one poorer than the last, until they hit the outskirts of the city of strip malls and low rise office blocks.

The sedan entered an underground car park beneath an unfinished office development. Concrete pillars stretched into darkness. The driver stopped beside a freight lift. “Could you leave your phone and any other wearable technology on the seat please sir. It will be waiting for you when you come back.” He didn’t turn around, just looked at his passenger in the rear-view mirror. Viktor could see were two cobalt blue eyes staring at him. He motioned with a raise of his eyebrows for Viktor to comply. Viktor made an exaggerated gesture of laying his phone on the car seat. The driver smiled. “Thank you sir, Level four.” Kosa stepped out.

The lift ascended slowly: one floor, two, three, four. The doors opened onto silence. FC Korvane’s invitation had arrived three weeks earlier. No email. No phone call. Just a cream envelope delivered to his home by a courier who never gave a name. It was titled: ‘A conversation regarding your future.’ Nothing more. Just a date, three weeks hence, a time, and details about the car picking him up from his door.

A single corridor stretched ahead beneath dim industrial lights. At the end stood a solid steel door. No signs gave a hint of what this place was or intended to be. There were no cameras visible, which meant there were cameras everywhere. Viktor reaching the metal door and knocked twice. Heavy, thumping knocks of a tough man using his fist and breathing deep to control his emotions. The door opened. The big defender was beckoned in.

Inside sat three men arranged in comfy chairs. A fourth sat empty, awaiting its participant. Each looked towards Viktor as he entered the room. One was immediately recognisable. Henrik Falk looked exactly like the newspapers described him: expensive suit, silver hair, expression carved from granite. The second was younger, casually dressed. The third remained watched in a way that suggested he was used to tracking prey of many kinds.

Falk gestured towards an empty chair. “Captain?” Kosa sat. A crystal glass appeared before him. Whisky. He didn’t touch it. Falk smiled faintly. “Good. I prefer cautious people.”

“You invited me here.”

“And you came.”

“Yeah, well curiosity is an interesting thing.”

“Ambition is more interesting Viktor.” The chairman’s smile widened.

“Sometimes they’re the same thing.”

Falk slid a folder across the table. No club crest. No branding. Just a summary and some numbers. The summary was short, the numbers large, very large indeed. Kosa glanced briefly before closing it. “You’re offering me too much.”

“Not for what we need.”

“And what exactly do you need?”

The third man, the man Viktor had christened ‘the hunter’ finally moved. It was a familiar face. Former national team manager Dusan Markovic. Kosa felt genuine surprise. Markovic lit a cigarette before he spoke.

“We need a statement.”

“A statement?”

“Yes, a player-coach that shows we mean business.” The smoke drifted lazily towards the ceiling.

Falk leaned forward. “FC Korvane has money. Facilities. Infrastructure. Everything except identity.”

“That’s not my problem.”

“It could be. We would like it to be.”

Silence settled across the room. Markovic spoke again. “You know what happens next, Viktor.”

Only old teammates called him Viktor. The familiarity wasn’t accidental. “Your legs still work,” Markovic continued. “But not forever.” Kosa remained expressionless. “One injury. One bad season. One slow recovery. One scandal. You are at an age now where you are one false step away from your career ending.” Each sentence landed with uncomfortable accuracy. “You need to start thinking like a coach.” The former manager stubbed out his cigarette then looked up at Viktor and smiled. “We can help with that.”

Viktor knew what he said was true. The future had been creeping towards him for years. Falk opened another folder. This one contained organisational charts. Training structures. Academy plans. Coaching pathways. Not the usual recruitment presentation. This looked like succession planning.

“We’re not looking to buy a defender,” Falk said, “we are looking to buy a leader for the next ten years.”

Outside the rain intensified, lashing against the windows. A Valrekian summer they called it.

Somewhere far beyond, thunder rolled. Kosa studied the documents carefully. Head Coach Development Programme. Technical Director Mentorship. Guaranteed UEFA licensing support.

Control. Influence. A future. Things footballers rarely received. Most were discarded when their usefulness ended. Viktor was being offered a future and more.

Markovic seemed to read his thoughts. “Dynamo sees you as captain.”

“Of course.”

“Korvane sees you as a future manager.”

The room grew quiet again. Dangerously quiet. Because beneath the offer sat another reality. Dynamo Bresno belonged to powerful people. People who didn’t enjoy losing assets. People who monitored conversations. Tracked relationships. Collected favours. The kind of people who rarely appeared in photographs but somehow always knew where you had been. Kosa looked directly at Falk.

“If this meeting becomes public…”

“It won’t.”

“If it does.”

The chairman shrugged. “Then it becomes a rumour.”

“And if Dynamo believes otherwise?”

Falk’s eyes hardened. “They can believe whatever they wish.”

For the first time all evening, Kosa smiled. Not because he was reassured. Because he understood. Everyone in the room knew exactly how dangerous this discussion was. That was why it had happened here. Why phones had been surrendered downstairs. Why no contracts existed. Why no assistants or agents were present. This wasn’t a negotiation. It was reconnaissance. Two sides measuring each other in the dark. Falk rose from his chair.

“The decision isn’t tonight.”

“Good.”

“But it will need to be made soon. We want this to be possible before the season starts.” Kosa stood. Falk extended his hand. “The game ends for every player.” Kosa shook it.

“Maybe.”

“The smart ones choose when.”

The captain turned and walked towards the door. No signatures. No photographs. No witnesses.

Just a conversation hidden in a city built on secrets. As the lift carried him back towards the car, he looked at his reflection in the steel doors. Captain of Dynamo Bresno. Leader. Club legend. Figurehead and symbol. Soon, perhaps, something else. The doors opened. Rain into the parking structure. The black sedan was waiting exactly where he had left it. Engine running. Driver silent. Kosa climbed inside. The car disappeared into the night.

And somewhere, in another part of Valreka, someone would already be trying to discover what had been discussed behind that locked door. In football, secrets were like blood in water. Eventually, something always came looking for them.
